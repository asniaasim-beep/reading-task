Reading study · stimulus & design analysis

The rung 2–3 hinge

The self-blame ladder is not a gradient. Forty-seven per cent of it happens in a single step — and that step is currently indistinguishable from sentence length.

Source · reading_study_engine.html 10 cells · 2 frames × 5 rungs

01 — What varies

The design is clean, and that makes it readable

Reading the sealed STIMULI object straight out of the engine: across all ten cells the cause, scene and neutral sentences are literally one string each. Only the self-blame sentence moves across rungs; only the question wording moves across frames. One trial per person, four sentences in a random order, pick one.

So whatever the ladder does, it does it through five sentences. Here they are.

02 — The core

The ladder has a hinge in it

I coded all five on six grammatical dimensions — what is predicated, over what span of time, with how much commitment, across how wide a set of people, with how much agency left in the subject, and whether the sentence still refers to the birthday at all. The step from (2) to (3) is 2.75 of the ladder's 5.85 total — 47% of the whole thing in one move. A principal components decomposition agrees: depth really is close to one dial (PC1 takes 93.9% of the variance), but the spacing along it is +0.36, +1.20, +0.48, +0.34.

Rungs 1 and 2 are still about the birthday. Rungs 3, 4 and 5 are about the person. Rung 1 says the invitation; rung 2 says I forgot to include. Neither can be understood without the cause sentence. From rung 3 on, Anna is gone, the birthday is gone, the invitation is gone — good people, lovable, alone refer to nothing in the paragraph.

That is not a difference of degree in how bad the writer feels. It is a change in what kind of sentence it is. Rungs 1–2 elaborate the causal story. Rungs 3–5 are a rival story, competing with the cause sentence to be what the paragraph is about. Which is why the response curve should be a step, not a line.

Because paragraph order is shuffled, in roughly half the orders the bound sentence precedes the cause sentence it depends on, making the dependency cataphoric. That processing cost lands only on rungs 1–2, and it is randomised rather than controlled.

03 — What it is built from

Each dimension is a known piece of machinery

  • σ is the stage-level / individual-level distinction. Rung 1's complement denotes an event. By (4) the predicate is copular and individual-level; by (5) it is -able — inherent potential. Not lovable is not something that can be true of a moment.
  • δ is genericity. Her → one of my closest friends (a partitive, which evokes a set) → good people (bare plural, kind-level) → the arbitrary implicit subject of to hold on to: anyone.
  • ε is complement selection, and it is the most carefully built dimension. Wish + past perfect asserts nothing. Feel stupid that P presupposes P but hedges the property. Wonder if embeds an interrogative — nothing is asserted at all. Believe commits. Certain that commits fully. Read in series, the writer's grip on the claim tightens clause by clause.
  • α is voice and morphology, and it is the quietest dimension. -able is passive-potential: lovable means able to be loved, so the self is the patient of an unrealised action by unnamed others. The grammar drains agency out of the subject as the ladder climbs, then deserve to be alone delivers a verdict of which the self is merely the recipient.

Where the frame manipulation does not reach

The setaside question is "Setting aside how it makes the writer feel, which is the most important sentence…". Two grammatical problems. First, it has no antecedent — the reader must invent one. Second, and much worse: the instruction names a feeling, and the top of the ladder is not feelings. Rung 2 is literally I feel stupid — a perfect lexical match. But rungs 3–5 are belief reports: wonder if, starting to believe, certain that. A belief that you are unlovable is not a feeling, and a reader complying precisely has no grounds to set it aside.

So the manipulation is strongest exactly where its lexical hook lands, and weakest exactly where the construct is most extreme. That is either a confound or the sharpest hypothesis in the study, depending entirely on whether you declare it in advance. Declare it.

04 — The problem

The hinge is collinear with sentence length

This is the most serious finding here. The cause sentence — the one the self-blame sentence competes against — is 14 words.

Since "pick the most important sentence" is known to be pulled by length and informativeness, a step at 2→3 is currently unattributable — depth and length predict the identical pattern, and no amount of N will separate them.

Two sentences that break it

Both written and word-counted to cross the two factors:

  • episode-bound 17 words, matching rung 3 — "I keep going over the day I wrote the guest list, wondering how I missed her name."
  • person-bound 12 words, matching rung 1 — "I have started to think I am simply not someone people keep."

Two extra cells, no engine change beyond the stimulus object, and length and depth become orthogonal.

05 — The maths

It is a choice experiment, not a 2×5 ANOVA

One trial, four alternatives, pick one. The matching estimator is McFadden's conditional logit. Depth enters as a shift in the utility of the self-blame option only, because that is the only option the manipulation touches.

V_ij = a_role(j) + p_pos(j) + 1[role(j)=B] · (b0 + b_d·d(r) + b_f·f + b_x·d(r)·f) P(pick j) = exp(V_ij) / Σ_k exp(V_ik)

This buys three things a chi-square on picked_is_cause does not: serial position is absorbed as a nuisance term instead of inflating error, the other three options stay in the model, and b_x — the claim from section 03 — becomes an actual parameter.

d(r) is where the linguistics becomes arithmetic. Three codings are three rival theories: linear (r−1)/4 says depth is a dial; step 1[r ≥ 3] says it is a switch; four free dummies let the data say. Both restricted codings nest inside the free one, so the shape is testable directly.

The estimator is sound — simulated and re-fitted, it recovers the true position and role parameters to two decimals, and its false-positive rate under a flat truth is nominal (.030, .027, .047). The depth effect is nearly free. The frame effect needs about N = 1500. The interaction is at 47% power even at N = 2000.

Read the rows, not the columns. If the instruction merely weakens at depth, this design cannot detect it at any budget you would plausibly spend. If it stops working entirely, you have .70 power at N = 1000 and .93 at N = 2000.

And that is the payoff. The grammatical argument — that "setting aside how it makes the writer feel" has no purchase on wonder if, believe, certain that — does not predict gentle attenuation. It predicts the instruction failing outright. That is the one version of the interaction this study can afford to test.

In percentage points the frame effect is flat and you write "no interaction". In odds it is cut by more than a third and you write "the instruction loses its grip at depth". Same data. The logit reports the second because it works in log odds. Neither is wrong, but the one that matches the hypothesis is the odds one. Decide which scale the claim lives on before you look.

A translation table is a claim, so the repo tests it. rosetta_check.py builds the design matrix twice — once by letting SQLite reconstruct the choice sets, once in Python straight from the wide row — and asserts the tensors are identical, the fitted coefficients differ by zero, and SQL's AVG() and Python's .mean() agree to machine precision. On 1,413 synthetic participants it passes.

The load-bearing insight is in the SQL: the saved wide row is a compressed choice set. pos1_role…pos4_role plus picked_position is exactly enough to reconstruct every option's role, its serial position and — by joining the extracted stimuli — its text. Nothing about the design is lost at save time, which means you never need to re-run anyone.

07 — Before recruiting

Things to fix

1

Break the length confound

Add the two sentences in section 04. Without them, a step at 2→3 has two equally good explanations and the study cannot separate them.

2

Two live engines, two destinations

reading_study_engine.html writes to DataPipe rrW097Bp4DWq with the real completion code. reading_study_study.html writes to xTRjQwQBv3yi with PLACEHOLDER-CODE, and assigns conditions with client-side Math.random() instead of the balanced rotation. Anyone reaching the wrong file cannot submit on Prolific. Archive it.

3

The assignment fallback is invisible

If getCondition throws, the engine falls back to Math.random() — correct behaviour, but nothing in the saved row records which path was taken. One line: add assign_method.

4

Every value is saved as a string

The row is built with String(v), so picked_is_cause is the four characters true, not 1. A natural df[df.picked_is_cause == True] returns nothing, silently.

5

Consider the failure mode at save

The save runs before the completion-code screen, which is the right order for data safety — but if it throws, the participant never sees the code and cannot submit. A fallback costs you a reconcilable payment instead of a support ticket and a lost participant.

08 — If it were mine

What I would do next

  1. Add the two de-confounding sentences. Everything else is downstream of this.
  2. Pre-register the hinge as primary, tested as rungs 1–2 vs 3–5 on P(cause). It reaches .92 power at N = 1000 as a plain two-proportion test, and it is the claim the grammar actually makes.
  3. Demote the interaction to secondary and say in advance it is underpowered — or reallocate to a 2×2 and buy it properly. Do not run a 2×5 at N = 500 and interpret the interaction; at 10% power a significant result there is more likely noise than signal.
  4. Fix the scale question before unblinding. Points or odds. Write it down.
  5. Code the why_text against the same six dimensions. It is the only channel where a reader says what they thought they were doing — and the natural place to test whether someone who picks self-blame at rung 5 is doing a different task than someone who picks it at rung 1.
